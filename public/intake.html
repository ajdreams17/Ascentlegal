07 · Filing Basis

Are you currently using the mark in commerce?

USPTO requires a filing basis. Use in commerce means you've already sold the goods/services across state lines (or with foreign customers) under the mark. Intent-to-use means you plan to but haven't yet.

Please choose your filing basis.
08 · Filing Basis

When did you first use the mark?

USPTO needs two dates: when the mark was first used anywhere, and first used in commerce (across state lines or with foreign customers). If unsure, give your best estimate — we can refine before filing.

If you don't have a specimen ready, we'll follow up with examples of what works (e.g., product packaging, website checkout pages, advertising, screenshots).

Tell us about your intent to use.

Since you're filing on intent-to-use, USPTO doesn't need first-use dates yet. We just need to confirm a bona fide intent to use the mark. We'll follow up later (after the application is allowed) to file a Statement of Use with specimen evidence.

A bona fide intent typically means you've done more than just have an idea — concrete steps like business plans, product development, marketing prep, or supply contracts help support the filing.

Tell us about your foreign filing.

Filing under §44 lets you base a U.S. application on a corresponding foreign application (§44(d) priority claim) or registration (§44(e)). Please provide the foreign details.

A §44(d) priority claim must be made within 6 months of the foreign filing date. We'll confirm timing and translate any non-English certificates as needed.

10 · Signature

Who is authorized to sign the application?

USPTO requires a signed declaration under penalty of perjury. The signatory must be the applicant (if individual) or someone authorized to bind the entity.

Please provide a signatory name and confirm the declaration.
